## Motivation

So the reason I picked this topic: I worked part-time at a Coop for about a year and a half while studying, mostly on the tills and later helping out at the self-checkout area. What I noticed is that some customers walk straight to the self-checkout every single time, and others refuse to touch it even when there's a long queue at the staffed till. Older customers especially. And then there's this whole thing where people get frustrated when the machine says "unexpected item in bagging area" and a staff member has to come over anyway, which kind of defeats the point.

I found this really interesting because from a Digital Business angle it's a classic technology adoption question — the tech works fine, but whether people actually use it depends on totally other stuff. Convenience, trust, feeling watched, not wanting to "do the shop's work for free", etc. I want to figure out what actually drives it in the Swiss grocery context, because most of the studies I found are from the US or UK.

## Research Questions

1. What are the main factors that motivate Swiss grocery shoppers to use self-checkout systems?
2. What are the main reasons shoppers avoid self-checkout and prefer a staffed checkout?
3. Do these factors differ by age group and by how often people shop?

## Planned Method

I'm thinking a mix:

- **Online survey** — spread through friends, family, uni channels, maybe a QR flyer if the store lets me. Aiming for maybe 100+ responses. Questions based on the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) — perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, plus some extra stuff on trust and "social" discomfort.
- **2 store manager interviews** — one at the Coop I worked at, and I'll try to get a second one at a Migros nearby. Semi-structured, like 30 min each. To get the retailer side: why they installed them, what problems they see, adoption numbers if they'll share.

Then descriptive stats + maybe a simple regression on the survey, and thematic notes from the interviews.
